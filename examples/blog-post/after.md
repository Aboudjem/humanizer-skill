I've been messing around with AI image generators for about six months now. The outputs are technically impressive — I got a portrait last week that looked better than anything I could paint in a year. But there's something missing. It's like eating a perfect-looking meal that has no flavor.

The tools are getting easier to use. You don't need a background in art or design. You type a description, press a button, and get something that would've taken a professional days. That's genuinely new. It didn't exist five years ago.

What's less clear is what happens to the people who used to get paid for that work. Some of them are adapting — using AI to speed up their process, doing in an hour what took a week. Others are not. I've talked to a few illustrators who've lost steady freelance clients in the last year. Not all of them, and not suddenly, but the trend is obvious.

None of this is resolved yet. People are still arguing about authorship, training data, compensation. The platforms keep shipping features and the courts keep getting served papers. What happens next depends a lot on decisions being made right now that most of us aren't part of.

I don't think the outcome is predetermined. But I also don't think "the future looks bright" is an analysis. It's a way of avoiding one.
